## 1. Purpose and scope

ASF's proposed operating commitment is to connect women with surfing, education, mutual support, and community without selling access to essential information or turning members into advertising inventory.

This charter governs organizational messaging, content decisions, supporter acknowledgment, donations, partnerships, member-benefit disclosures, membership explanations, safeguarding, and accountability. It applies to leadership, volunteer coordinators, writers, translators, moderators, and anyone negotiating or communicating on ASF's behalf.

It does not by itself authorize expenditure, sign contracts, appoint officers, operate a reporting service, change registration processes, amend a statute, or modify an application. Existing legal obligations and governing documents must be checked before adoption. Any conflict with them must be resolved through the proper process, not silently overridden by this draft.

### Proposed public summary in Portuguese

**Use only after adoption and verification of practice:**

> A ASF aproxima mulheres do surf por meio de comunidade, educação e apoio mútuo. Nossos conteúdos e ferramentas comunitárias são gratuitos, sem anúncios, assinatura paga ou paywall. Apoios e doações não compram recomendações editoriais, dados das participantes nem prioridade de atendimento. Produtos, aulas, viagens e outros serviços opcionais de parceiros podem ter custos próprios, informados diretamente por cada responsável.

This statement must not be published as an existing operational guarantee until the relevant owners can support it.

## 2. Zero-paywall and zero-subscription standard

After adoption:

1. Public educational content, safety information, and the core community tools identified by leadership must not require a paid subscription, premium plan, purchase, donation, referral, advertising interaction, or points redemption for access.
2. Safety limitations, help, complaints information, and privacy explanations must never be locked behind progress, points, purchases, or membership rewards.
3. Free registration or proportionate community access checks may be used for legitimate privacy, moderation, or membership administration. They must be explained accurately and must not be disguised payment barriers.
4. ASF must not imply that a free digital card renews into a paid subscription. Issuance, validity, renewal, and any formal membership obligations must be stated before registration.
5. Donations must be genuinely optional and must not buy access to ordinary community participation, rankings, favorable editorial coverage, or expedited handling of a safeguarding complaint.
6. No writer or partner may describe a future feature, reward, mentoring session, or competition place as available unless its availability, responsible provider, and conditions have been confirmed.
7. This charter does not promise that every external product, lesson, trip, accommodation, or event is free. Such optional costs must be distinguished from free ASF content and tools.

If ASF's actual statutes impose dues or different membership categories, leadership must resolve that issue before adopting or publicizing a zero-fee membership promise. This draft is not an amendment to those statutes.

## 3. Zero-advertising standard

Advertising is assessed by its substance, not its label. Under this proposed standard, ASF must not sell or exchange access to its audience for product persuasion, sales acquisition, preferential recommendations, or purchased prominence.

### Prohibited arrangements

- Display advertising networks, automated ad placements, retargeting, or behavior-based advertising.
- Sponsored articles, paid recommendations, paid rankings, or undisclosed advertorials.
- Guaranteed impressions, clicks, leads, customers, logo exposure across applications, or promotional delivery quotas sold as inventory.
- Affiliate commissions, payment per sale or sign-up, or rewards tied to members clicking promotional links or making purchases.
- Product placement inside safety, health, educational, registration, or support journeys in exchange for consideration.
- Sponsored direct messages, access to member lists, location-based commercial targeting, or transfer of individual member activity for a supporter's commercial purposes.
- Naming, exclusivity, or visibility arrangements that materially restrict editorial independence or community access. Existing arrangements require review rather than automatic validation.

A consent banner does not create an exception to the zero-ad promise. If leadership later considers an advertising model, it must treat that as a material policy change, follow the proper approval process, assess legal and privacy implications, and correct the public promise before presenting the new model as consistent with zero advertising.

This document does not assert that any specific advertising arrangement is currently active.

## 4. Ethical supporter acknowledgment

Neutral recognition can thank a supporter without selling promotion. An acknowledgment must meet **all** of these tests:

1. **Factual:** identifies the supporter and the nature or period of support without slogans, comparative claims, promotional prices, or purchase pressure.
2. **Bounded:** appears in an appropriate support, credits, or transparency context, not as a repeated interruption inside member tasks.
3. **Independent:** does not purchase editorial choice, product endorsement, audience access, favorable treatment, or silence about legitimate concerns.
4. **Proportionate:** presentation does not imply a guaranteed exposure package, a recommended supplier ranking, or certification of a product.
5. **Transparent:** material financial or in-kind support and relevant conflicts are disclosed in an understandable way.
6. **Privacy-respecting:** any neutral link does not conceal tracking or transmit member information for commercial targeting.
7. **Approved:** the responsible, non-conflicted decision-makers have recorded why the arrangement meets this charter.

Examples of proposed acceptable wording:

- PT-BR: Agradecemos a [nome] pela doação de [tipo de apoio] para [atividade], em [período]. O apoio não interfere nas decisões editoriais da ASF.
- EN: We thank [name] for contributing [type of support] to [activity] during [period]. Support does not determine ASF's editorial decisions.

The bracketed fields are editorial placeholders, not facts to publish without confirmation. A logo is not automatically acceptable merely because it appears beside the word supporter; its context, agreement, prominence, and purpose must pass the same test. These are governance criteria, not a legal or tax classification of sponsorship.

## 5. Separate partnership categories

| Category | Permitted purpose under the proposal | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Unrestricted donation | Support the mission without purchasing commercial access. | No editorial or member-access entitlement. |
| Restricted donation | Support a defined activity consistent with the mission. | Accepted restrictions must be recorded, feasible, and honored; no diversion without proper authorization. |
| In-kind support | Provide useful goods, facilities, expertise, or services. | Suitability and value assessment must be reasonable; no unsupported efficacy claims or compulsory member promotion. |
| Neutral supporter acknowledgment | Factually thank a supporter. | Must pass all tests in section 4. |
| Member-benefit arrangement | Give participants an optional, verified practical benefit. | Separate from advertising inventory; no commission, paid ranking, or obligation to buy. |
| Independent editorial reference | Cite a source or discuss a relevant example. | Selected for educational value, with conflicts disclosed; not paid content. |
| Commercial promotion | Purchase prominence, persuasion, acquisition, or targeting. | Not permitted under the proposed zero-ad standard. |

Partner status does not make a provider an ASF-certified instructor, clinician, safety authority, or guaranteed supplier. Professional qualifications and claims must be checked where relevant; a listing is not a guarantee of treatment, performance, safety, availability, or results.

## 6. Partner and donor approval process

Before any announcement, a designated owner must prepare a brief record containing:

- Identity of the organization or individual, verified contact, and the proposed relationship.
- Purpose, intended beneficiaries, geography, duration, and activities involved.
- Money, goods, services, permissions, or other consideration exchanged.
- Any proposed public acknowledgment, member offer, link, image use, or access request.
- Conflicts of interest, safeguarding concerns, complaints history relevant to the decision, and capacity to meet the arrangement.
- Proposed public wording and any evidence supporting a metric or claim.
- Approval status, responsible people, review date, and termination or correction route.

A partnership must be approved in writing by the appropriate non-conflicted authority and accepted by both parties before public announcement. The person negotiating an arrangement must not be its only approver. A small organization may combine roles, but it must preserve an independent second review for conflicts, resources, and public claims.

No one may promise a response time, event place, service, deliverable, or exposure commitment beyond ASF's demonstrated capacity and authorized agreement.

### Due-diligence exclusions and escalation

Reject arrangements involving illegal conduct, discrimination, harassment, exploitation, deceptive claims, misuse of personal information, or interference with participant safety. Higher-risk categories and offers involving children, health claims, gambling, alcohol, or financial products require explicit leadership consideration and appropriate advice; some may be unsuitable regardless of funding value. Tobacco and vaping promotion is incompatible with this proposed health- and community-oriented standard.

Accepting support does not entitle a donor to identify vulnerable participants, contact children, obtain private stories, or require publicity from beneficiaries.

## 7. Transparent member-benefit listings

A benefit is publishable only after its terms and permission to disclose them are confirmed. Each listing must state, as applicable:

1. Partner and relationship type.
2. Whether the beneficiary is an individual member, a partner business, or another clearly defined group.
3. What the offer actually provides; no implied universal benefit.
4. Eligibility, including the meaning of a valid card if required.
5. Dates, availability, booking or redemption requirements, exclusions, and any minimum purchase.
6. What the participant pays, or a clear instruction to confirm the final price directly.
7. Who supplies and contracts for the service and who handles changes, cancellation, refunds, and complaints.
8. Whether ASF receives support and whether that support is connected to the relationship.
9. Last confirmation date, responsible ASF role, and route to report inaccurate information.

Unconfirmed offers must remain clearly identified as pending internally, not presented as active discounts. An expired or disputed offer must be reviewed promptly and not knowingly promoted as current.

A discount on an external business subscription is not an ASF subscription. A benefit for partner businesses must not be advertised as a universal member discount.

Coupons may be public or private according to the written agreement and participant-protection needs. Do not publish a code without permission; equally, do not assume that every already-public code was unauthorized. Never publish a member's card image or personal information as proof of eligibility. Explain any information a participant must provide directly to a partner before they do so.

## 8. Payments, gifts, and use of resources

The proposed model preserves the public distinction that members contract and pay directly with partners. ASF does not intermediate partner purchases, reservations, or refunds under this charter. Any future proposal for ASF to collect activity fees or intermediate transactions needs separate authority, financial controls, legal review where appropriate, and clear public communication; this charter does not authorize it.

Donations and in-kind support require an internal record of purpose, date, source, conditions, responsible custodian, and use. Financial records and reconciliations must be reviewed by an appropriately independent person. Restricted support must be applied to its accepted purpose or handled through a documented return or variation decision.

Do not promise tax deductibility, donation receipts with a particular legal effect, or a financial benefit without verifying ASF's status and the applicable rules.

Publish a proportionate, understandable summary of support and use of resources at a cadence leadership can sustain. Protect legitimate donor privacy and participant dignity. Public transparency does not require publishing bank details, personal identifiers, confidential agreements, or individual member records. Anonymous public donations still require appropriate internal accountability.

Gifts, prizes, raffles, and contests require their own eligibility, consent, selection, fulfillment, privacy, and legal review. Do not assume that being a nonprofit exempts an activity from applicable promotion or prize rules. This charter does not launch or approve a contest.

## 9. Conflicts of interest and independence

Leadership, coordinators, reviewers, and negotiators must disclose relevant financial, family, employment, referral, or other relationships before a decision. The affected person must not be the sole decision-maker and should step out of approval where the conflict requires it. Record the conflict and how it was managed.

No donor or partner may:

- Select favorable learning conclusions or suppress corrections.
- Require members to buy, post endorsements, reveal health information, or consent to publicity as a condition of ordinary participation.
- Obtain private member data or privileged access to complaints.
- Influence a safeguarding response or retaliate against a participant.
- Use ASF's name or logo beyond the agreed permission or imply a broader endorsement.

Termination or refusal of support must be possible when mission, independence, safety, or privacy is compromised. Existing contractual obligations require proper review; this draft does not purport to cancel an agreement.

## 10. Membership, registration, and Carteirinha clarity

ASF must distinguish these concepts rather than using member for all of them interchangeably:

| Concept | Explanation leadership must confirm |
|---|---|
| Public reader | Access to free information without implying statutory membership. |
| Community participant | Participation subject to proportionate conduct, consent, and access rules. |
| Registered cardholder | A person registered through the confirmed card process, with stated validity and benefit conditions. |
| Formal association member | Any legal category, rights, duties, admission process, voting rights, or dues defined by the actual governing documents. |

A card must not automatically be described as conferring formal voting rights, insurance, professional competence, or safety approval. If it does confer particular rights, those rights must be verified and explained plainly.

Before adoption, the membership owner must reconcile the current public issuance and recovery copy with the Privacy Policy's description of local and optional central registration. The explanation must identify the actual process, not a preferred future process.

Member-facing information must cover cost, eligibility, required and optional information, email-confirmation purpose, validity, renewal, expiry, recovery, help, and the limited information exposed by any verification function. Writers must not change the meaning of an actual process through reassuring but unsupported wording.

## 11. Privacy and consent accountability

Privacy explanations must be specific to each activity. A statement that Diário entries stay in a browser must not become a claim that every ASF activity stays on the device. Nor should technical details be used as a substitute for answering who receives information, why, for how long, and how someone asks for help.

The designated privacy owner must confirm:

- The information collected for each activity and whether it is necessary or optional.
- Purposes, responsible parties, applicable legal bases, recipients, storage, retention, and access arrangements.
- Separate choices for optional publicity, marketing, images, or location where applicable.
- A practical route for requests, correction, withdrawal where applicable, and deletion subject to valid obligations.
- What is public, what remains internal, what is shared directly with a partner by the participant, and what is not shared by ASF.

Consent must be understandable and specific where required. Do not bundle optional publicity or location sharing into necessary card administration. Do not promise absolute security, anonymity, confidentiality, or deletion without confirming the relevant limits.

A no-ad policy does not automatically mean no cookies, no technical logs, or no third-party services. Describe verified essential functions and any independently justified optional measurement accurately. Do not retain an advertising permission merely as a vague future option while claiming zero advertising.

No private membership lists, identity documents, consent evidence, safeguarding reports, or sensitive operational records belong in these public Markdown documents or the public repository.

## 12. Safeguarding, dignity, and community participation

The proposed community standard respects women across backgrounds, bodies, ages, abilities, sexual orientations, and gender histories, including trans women. Leadership must clearly and respectfully define eligibility for particular programs, youth activities, and formal membership, rather than leaving people to infer it from appearance or language.

### Minimum participation protections

- Clear conduct rules against harassment, discrimination, sexual misconduct, coercion, bullying, scams, and retaliation.
- Visible reporting and removal-request routes, with a backup when a report concerns the primary responsible person.
- Clear expectations for who can see posts, photographs, profile details, or location information.
- Permission for people pictured and an appropriate child-protection process before publishing images of minors.
- No collection of public medical histories as a condition of belonging.
- A distinction between peer companionship and qualified instruction, treatment, rescue, or supervision.
- Clear explanation that ASF is not an emergency service or a continuously monitored crisis channel.

For imminent danger, participants should contact local emergency services or on-site lifeguards directly. Moderators must not promise emergency response that ASF cannot provide. Child participation requires an appropriate program-specific safeguarding framework and guardian involvement where required; an adult card process is not a child-safeguarding protocol.

### Proposed reporting procedure, to operationalize before adoption

1. Receive the concern through a designated, privacy-conscious route and explain confidentiality limits.
2. Assess immediate safety and refer emergencies to appropriate services; do not attempt an unqualified investigation or rescue.
3. Route the matter to the safeguarding owner or an independent backup if there is a conflict.
4. Take proportionate protective moderation measures, preserve necessary information securely, and avoid public accusations or demands for repeated disclosure.
5. Follow applicable reporting duties and obtain appropriate advice; do not promise secrecy that law or protection duties cannot support.
6. Explain next steps and available review routes, document the decision securely, and prevent retaliation.

Leadership must set realistic acknowledgment and follow-up targets based on trained capacity and publish only the service it can actually sustain. No target is operational merely because it appears in a draft. A general contact address must not be presented as a dedicated, continuously monitored safeguarding channel without an accountable owner and backup.

## 13. Safety-responsible editorial governance

Educational and health-related content must distinguish general information, forecasts, individual advice, and lived experience. A broad disclaimer does not justify a harmful instruction or an unsupported efficacy claim.

Require appropriate specialist review before publishing or reusing material about water safety, breath-hold practices, injury, rehabilitation, nutrition prescriptions, or mental-health treatment. Do not generalize a founder's experience into a clinical promise.

Do not encourage eyes-closed exercises in the water, breath-hold or submersion preparation, or fixed rules that label a sea state safe for a person. Do not treat a forecast, badge, card, number of sessions, board formula, or points total as proof of readiness.

Community recognition should favor learning, respectful behavior, reflection, appropriate rest, and seeking qualified advice. It must not pressure people to remain in the water longer, ignore fatigue, maintain unsafe attendance streaks, or purchase partner products.

## 14. Roles and decision rights

These are functions to assign, not claims that the positions currently exist.

| Function | Main accountability | Approval or escalation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized leadership body | Adopt policy, oversee resources and risk, resolve material conflicts. | Cannot delegate away ultimate oversight; must follow actual governing documents. |
| Editorial coordinator | Maintain inventory, sources, status, corrections, and review dates. | Escalates legal, safety, privacy, or policy conflicts before publication. |
| Membership/privacy owner | Confirm registration explanations and data-handling notices. | Obtains appropriate advice; cannot invent a process through copy. |
| Partnerships steward | Verify offers, permission, consideration, and renewal information. | Cannot approve their own conflicted arrangement alone. |
| Finance reviewer | Record and review support and restrictions. | Preserves appropriate independence and access controls. |
| Safeguarding owner and backup | Handle concerns and referral routes with privacy and fairness. | Partner, donor, or seniority pressure must not determine outcomes. |
| Qualified subject reviewer | Review material within demonstrated competence. | Review is scoped; it is not blanket approval of all ASF content. |
| Translator or localization reviewer | Preserve meaning, limits, consent, and audience distinctions. | Must not strengthen a claim or broaden a permission in translation. |

Small-team combinations are acceptable only where capacity, conflicts, backups, and second-review requirements remain workable.

## 15. Evidence, corrections, and review

Every material public claim needs an owner, source, relevant period, review date, and approved wording. Metrics must state what was counted and when. Do not publish invented precision, unverifiable superlatives, guaranteed reach, or guaranteed member outcomes.

Maintain a public-facing content status and an internal decision trail appropriate to the information. Historical monetization documents must be clearly distinguished from current policy if retained. The current funding position and public policies must not quietly contradict each other.

A correction should state what changed and why when the change is material, without repeating harmful instructions or revealing private information. Safety, privacy, or safeguarding concerns take precedence over promotional schedules. Stable content should receive a planned review; time-sensitive benefits should be reconfirmed on a suitable shorter cycle.

Review this charter at least annually, after a material incident, after changes to membership or data practices, or before changing the funding model. Material changes require the same appropriate authority and clear version history. A partner request or a banner choice cannot silently amend policy.
